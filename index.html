How to read the board

Two views of the same season

Halo uses 499,503 trips from 4,043 games whose reconstructed lineups passed minutes checks. SportsDataverse uses 875,943 possessions from 6,291 identified games. Among the 677 matched players, the ratings correlated at 0.82.

What does the forecast mean for a Stanford player?

The same question applies to every prospect: if he played at Cal next season, how likely would his total RAPM be to exceed the ACC benchmark? A Stanford player already has ACC experience. His full-season performance at Stanford supplies the starting point, and the model uses Cal as his destination.

Why scout for top-quarter impact?

The median identifies players who could improve on a typical ACC player. The top quarter sets a higher bar: impact above three of every four rated ACC players. Jeremy Fears Jr. leads the board at 91% to clear the median and 62% to clear the top-quarter cutoff. The model adds the range of errors it saw on past transfers to his forecast. About six in ten of those outcomes clear the higher bar.

Board percentile describes last season. A 90th percentile player finished ahead of about 90% of rated prospects in the selected dataset.

How well did it rank transfers?

The model ranked 202 transfers across three ACC classes using only earlier seasons to train each forecast. Players it rated higher generally posted higher total RAPM the next season. The correlation was 0.37 with destination strength held constant. In the most recent class, the 19 highest projected transfers averaged +4.30 in actual total RAPM. The 19 lowest averaged +0.45.
