Two views of the same season

The board includes 677 players with both RAPM estimates and ACC forecasts. Twenty-one players with unresolved revised-data matches were excluded.

Halo uses 499,503 trips from 4,043 games whose reconstructed lineups passed minutes checks. SportsDataverse uses 875,943 possessions from 6,291 identified games. Among the 677 matched players, the ratings correlated at 0.82.

When a player transfers within the ACC

For a player moving from one ACC school to Cal, last season’s total RAPM measures his impact at his previous school. The model uses that performance and Cal’s team strength to forecast his total RAPM next season. “Above median” is the chance his impact at Cal would exceed the midpoint among last season’s rated ACC players.

How well did it rank transfers?

Across 202 transfers in three ACC classes, the correlation between projected and actual total RAPM was 0.37 when every player in a class was evaluated at the same destination strength. Each forecast used only earlier classes for training. In the 2025–26 backtest, each of the 78 transfers was compared with every other transfer, counting each pair once. That produced 3,003 comparisons. In 64.5% of them (1,936 pairs), the player the model ranked higher also recorded the higher total RAPM after transferring. Ranking agreement was statistically significant in an exact player-level permutation test (two-sided p < 0.001).

How much possession data is enough?

A chronological 2024–25 study compared total RAPM from earlier games with rankings from later, withheld games. Higher possession screens produced stronger ranking agreement, but no tested cutoff met both prespecified requirements: at least 100 players and a 95% confidence interval entirely above a rank correlation of 0.30.

Intervals were estimated by resampling schools 2,000 times. The 0.30 criterion was a stated standard for repeatability. No cutoff qualified for the planned independent 2025–26 confirmation test, so the board retains its existing inclusion rules and 677 players. The possession screen is not a statistically validated guarantee of individual RAPM accuracy.

Study design and results

Candidate screens ranged from 500 to 2,000 possessions on each side. Earlier games supplied the ratings and exposure counts; later games supplied the comparison ratings, with at least 250 later possessions per side required. The national RAPM fit retained its penalty of 1,000. The earlier game-bootstrap study also found that shrinkage can make low-exposure estimates appear stable, so interval width alone was not used to select a cutoff.
